\textbf{Figure S2 $|$ End-to-end design and validation of the synthetic single-cell RNA-seq
data generator.}

\textbf{(A)}~Schematic of the cluster-plus-hub factor model used to define the gene-gene
correlation structure.
Each gene (blue nodes) belongs to a cluster whose members share variance through a
dedicated cluster hub (orange nodes).
A randomly selected subset of cluster hubs is additionally linked to a single global hub
(red star), introducing long-range correlation between clusters.
Edge weights are modulated by the shared-variance parameter $\chi$.

\textbf{(B,~C)}~Representative $100\times 100$ sub-blocks of correlation matrices generated
with high ($\chi = 0.9$, \textbf{B}) and low ($\chi = 0.5$, \textbf{C})
shared-variance fractions.
Block-diagonal structure reflects the cluster topology; off-diagonal inter-cluster
correlations arise through the global hub.

\textbf{(D)}~Flow diagram of count generation.
\textit{Left:} Correlated latent variables are drawn from a multivariate normal
distribution $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\,R)$.
\textit{Center:} Gene counts are obtained by mapping each latent value to a uniform
marginal with the standard normal CDF $\Phi$ and then applying the negative-binomial
quantile function $\mathrm{NB}^{-1}$; the per-gene mean $\mu_i$ is drawn from an
inverse-Gamma distribution to reproduce the heavy-tailed expression distribution observed
in real scRNA-seq data.
\textit{Right:} Cell-level library-size variability is introduced by multiplicative
log-normal amplitudes, followed by expression-dependent dropout
($P(\text{dropout}) = e^{-\lambda\,x}$) to generate the sparse observed count matrix;
the inset reports the overall fraction of zero entries.

\textbf{(E,~F)}~Rank plots of per-cell (\textbf{E}) and per-gene (\textbf{F}) total
expression, with rank in descending order of total expression on the $x$-axis and total
expression on a logarithmic $y$-axis.
Within each pair the simulated data (left) are shown alongside a representative real
dataset (right; \texttt{sample\_2b\_filtered.csv}) on a shared $y$-axis; the simulation is
sized to match the data ($1000$ cells $\times$ $2000$ genes) and uses the observed
(post-dropout) counts.
For the per-gene panels the single highest-expression gene is omitted so that the
remaining distribution is not compressed by one outlier.
Together these confirm that the simulation reproduces the heavy-tailed cell- and gene-level
total-expression distributions characteristic of real single-cell data.

\medskip
\noindent
The correlation heatmaps in row~1 are generated from matrices with $n = 2000$ genes,
Pareto shape parameter $s = 1.5$, and hub-connectivity probability
$p_\mathrm{hub} = 0.2$.
The count-generation schematic (row~2) uses a small
$n_\mathrm{cells} = n_\mathrm{genes} = 400$ simulation. The rank plots (row~3) use a
$1000 \times 2000$ simulation whose parameters mirror the GMP-Cor calibration sweep
($\chi = 0.9$, dropout rate $\lambda = 1$, inverse-Gamma shape/scale $1.5/0.01$), so the
simulated total-expression distributions track the real data.
