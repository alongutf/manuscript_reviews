INTERPRETATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Two dysregulated sub-populations that are transcriptomically opposite
  separate cleanly in UMAP and are bimodal along the group axis - the
  scenario the reviewer raises.

  Both sub-populations are individually at the noise floor, yet their
  mixture is strongly elevated - the separating mode alone accounts for
  almost all of it. Two conclusions follow:

  1. Mixing NEVER lowers GMP-Cor. Even mixing two populations that have
     no internal coordination at all raises the index well above what
     either shows alone. A low observed GMP-Cor therefore cannot be
     produced by population heterogeneity - which is exactly the
     reviewer's concern, answered from the opposite direction.
  2. The converse does NOT hold, and this bounds the claim: the raw
     index alone cannot separate a coordinated single population from a
     mixture of uncoordinated ones, since the two land in the same
     range. dGMP does separate them (see below), which is why it, and
     not the scalar, is the diagnostic to report for a clustered sample.

  Group-mean centering separates the two contributions: it removes the
  between-population mode while leaving within-cell coordination intact.
  dGMP is therefore a direct test on real data of whether an observed GMP-Cor
  is mixture structure or genuine coordination — a test that requires
  single-cell data and cannot be performed on bulk RNA-seq.
