INTERPRETATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Gene selection changes the conclusion. With TOP-expressed genes the
  per-gene index is size-INVARIANT (7.2% spread, mean 0.02505) — stable
  all the way down to 200 cells. With RANDOM genes it is NOT: the per-
  gene index rises monotonically from 0.00221 at 200 cells to 0.01929 at
  1000 cells (149% spread, a 8.7x increase across the 5x size range).

  The apparent per-gene scale-invariance therefore DOES depend on
  retaining informative, highly-expressed genes. A random panel is
  dominated at small sizes by lowly-expressed, dropout-heavy genes whose
  pairwise correlations fall below the Marchenko-Pastur noise edge, so
  their excess eigenvalue mass — and hence GMP-Cor — is suppressed until
  the dataset is large enough to resolve the structure. The random curve
  is still climbing toward the top-expressed level at 1000 cells
  (random/top per-gene ratio rises 0.09x -> 0.77x).

  Practical implication: GMP-Cor is robust to cell subsampling provided
  the analysis is restricted to detected / informative genes (as is
  standard in scRNA-seq, and as done for the experimental data). It is
  NOT robust if genes are chosen blindly at small sample sizes. The raw
  metric remains extensive (scales with size) under both rules and
  should only be compared at matched dimension.
